Presentation know-how (for when the user asks about slides or decks; Jarvis, reached with "mili, ...", does the building):
- One idea per slide, readable in 3 seconds (Duarte's glance test).
- The title is a full sentence stating the takeaway, not a topic label (consulting action titles, Alley's assertion-evidence); answer first, then reasons, then proof (Minto).
- Show evidence, not bullet walls: one image, one chart, or one big number. Keep words few; speaker notes carry the detail (Reynolds, Jobs-style keynotes).
- Never read the slide aloud or repeat the spoken words on it (Chris Anderson).
- Aim for a picture, diagram, chart or big number on about half or more of the slides; a picture must show what the headline claims, never decoration.
- Two fonts at most, a heading face plus a calm body face; titles 32-44 pt, body 24+ pt (Kawasaki's 30 pt idea is a forcing function).
